### Notable finding for the paper (not a protocol violation, flagged for Daniel)
The v1.0 protocol's Method 1 parameters (stop-words removed from bigrams) make
first-person phrase triangulation essentially impossible: phrases like
"we believe", "we expect", "we are confident" are generated by Method 5 but
cannot be corroborated by Method 1 because the stop-word filter removes "we"
and "I" from bigram candidates. Only `base rate` (Method 1 bigram + Method 5)
survived as a phrase. 20 Method-5-generated phrases were rejected as singletons.

This is **not** a protocol violation -- I followed the spec verbatim. But it is
a material limitation of the v1.0 protocol for phrase-level extension.
Options for Daniel to consider (NOT unilaterally applied):
1. Accept that phrase work is out-of-scope for v1.0 and focus on unigrams.
2. Amend the protocol to v1.1 with a narrow exception: bigrams may retain a
   leading first-person pronoun (`we`, `I`, `our`, `my`) even though these are
   stop-words for unigram purposes. This is a principled fix because the
   construct is explicitly about first-person epistemic framing.
3. Add a sixth method dedicated to first-person phrase mining (e.g., pattern
   match `^(we|I|our) \w+` with frequency >= 100 in corpus) so phrases can
   triangulate via two methods.
No action taken until Daniel decides.
